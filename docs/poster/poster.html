Real science, hidden in play.

Two 16-bit games for ages 5 to 99. Dodge ocean trash. Fly to Jupiter's icy moon. Every fact in them is real.

01 · WHYFacts stick when you play them.

The problem

A fact in a lesson stays a sentence

“Europa hides an ocean.” “Plastic hurts sea turtles.” A child hears it, and the next day it is gone. Facts feel far away from play.

Our solution

Make the fact a game moment

In Crab Quest you dodge the trash. In Space X you fly through Europa’s ice. Each fact shows up where it makes sense in the story, and nothing in it is invented.

02 · SEE ITTwo games, ten levels.

Space X, level 4: the Ship breaks Europa's ice with footballs. Each chunk needs two hits, and the Europa level ends with a 3-second scan.

  • Crab Quest: 6 chaptersPlatforming, dodging, collecting and surviving, from a kitchen to a coral sanctuary.
  • Space X: 4 levelsLaunch pad, Earth orbit, asteroid belt and Europa, then the win screen on Jupiter.
  • A fact card after every levelA story line and one science fact in Space X.
  • A planet fact on every game overThe Sun and all eight planets, each with a picture.
  • Keyboard or on-screen buttonsArrows, Space, P and R, plus clickable left, right and pause in Space X.
  • Music, mute and fullscreenA mute button in Crab Quest; pause and fullscreen (F) in Space X.
